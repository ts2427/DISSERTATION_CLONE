### Update: Authorship Confirmed for Oct-12 Item #10

#### Haapamäki, E. & Sihvonen, J. (2026)
**Title:** Mandatory cybersecurity disclosure: Early evidence from 10-K reports  
**Journal:** *International Journal of Accounting Information Systems*, Vol. 57 (2026)  
**DOI/URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1467089526000059  
**Open-access preprint:** https://osuva.uwasa.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/dffcef7c-7d76-4df1-9338-5947aa52a495/content  
**Publication Period:** 2026 (PRIMARY — confirmed this week)

**Summary:** Analyzes 3,440 Item 1C cybersecurity disclosures from 2024 annual 10-K filings. Finds that investors and analysts show minimal measurable response — no systematic price reaction, no increase in analyst cybersecurity discussion, and no rise in investor attention to annual reports. Disclosure length and specificity are driven by firm size, auditor quality, and peer practices, not by breach history. Concludes that strategic discretion persists even under mandatory disclosure.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H2 (FCC/SEC regulation impact on returns):** Core negative result for annual disclosure channel; provides important contrast with Item 1.05 incident disclosures; suggests regulators may need incident-level (not annual) mandates to move markets
- **H1 (firm characteristics):** Firm size drives disclosure quality, suggesting small-firm disclosure is systematically thinner — supports heterogeneous market reactions
- **Overall utility:** Pair with Block (2025) to distinguish annual vs. incident disclosure effects; "minimal response" finding is a key tension point for dissertation H2

#### 1. Gordon, L. A., Loeb, M. P., Tseng, C. Y. & Zhou, L. (2025)
**Title:** Cybersecurity Reporting: Preliminary Empirical Evidence on the Impact of Item 1C of 10-K Reports Filed with the SEC  
**Journal:** *Journal of Information Security* (Scientific Research Publishing)  
**DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.4236/jis.2025.164025  
**Publication Period:** 2025 (PRIMARY)

**Summary:** Examines the first wave of Item 1C cybersecurity management disclosures in 10-K filings (December 2023 – August 2024). Finds that firms which integrate cyber risk management into enterprise risk management (ERM) frameworks are significantly more likely to report material weaknesses in internal control, suggesting ERM integration may surface previously hidden vulnerabilities. Does not directly study stock price effects but addresses disclosure quality and governance linkages.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H2 (regulation impact):** Provides evidence on the operational (rather than market) effects of the SEC's mandatory annual disclosure; complements Haapamäki & Sihvonen on market outcomes
- **H6 (executive turnover/governance):** Material weakness disclosures are a governance failure signal that commonly precedes CEO/CFO departures — indirect H6 linkage
- **Overall utility:** Useful for framing the SEC regulation chapter; Gordon & Loeb are the canonical cybersecurity investment economists

#### 2. Authors TBD (2026)
**Title:** Reassessing the market impact of cyber incidents: A bias-adjusted event study approach  
**Journal:** *International Review of Economics & Finance* (Elsevier, 2026)  
**DOI/URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1059056026005010  
**Publication Period:** 2026 (PRIMARY)

**Summary:** Applies bias-corrected event study methods to a broad sample of cyber incidents and finds that standard approaches (OLS market model) significantly overstate price effects. After adjusting for methodological bias, ransomware attacks show no statistically significant stock impact; significant effects concentrate in healthcare-sector data breaches. Results challenge the robustness of prior event-study findings on ransomware.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H4 (breach type effects):** Core finding: breach type matters significantly — ransomware ≠ data breach in market terms; healthcare sector is a high-sensitivity outlier
- **H1 (firm characteristics/timing):** Bias adjustment reveals that prior heterogeneous firm-level results may partly reflect estimation artifacts
- **H5 (volatility/information asymmetry):** Methodological paper also addresses how ARCH effects and non-normality inflate volatility measures
- **Overall utility:** Important methodological contribution; cite when defending event-study design choices; challenges several prior "ransomware effect" findings

#### 3. Authors TBD (2025)
**Title:** The Dynamics of Stock Market Responses Following the Cyber-Attacks News: Evidence from Event Study  
**Journal:** *Information Systems Frontiers* (Springer, 2025)  
**DOI/URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10796-025-10639-6  
**Publication Period:** 2025 (PRIMARY)

**Summary:** Large-sample event study using a Fama-French three-factor model. Finds an average event-day CAR of approximately −0.15% and mean loss of ~$619 million, with more negative CARs for higher-severity attacks and for first-time targets (no prior breach history). Effects persist across alternative event windows. The severity-and-novelty gradient is a key contribution.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H3 (prior breach history penalty):** First-time targets show more negative reactions than repeat-breach firms — partially supports H3, but inverts the typical "penalty for repetition" framing; provides important nuance
- **H4 (breach type):** Severity gradient by attack type is tested
- **H5 (volatility):** Fama-French three-factor decomposition allows separating systematic vs. idiosyncratic return components
- **Overall utility:** Large recent sample; peer-reviewed Springer; good methodological anchor for the event-study chapter

#### 4. Authors TBD (2023)
**Title:** An Analysis of Cyber Breaches and Effects on Shareholder Wealth  
**Journal:** *International Journal of the Economics of Business*, Vol. 30, No. 1 (2023, Taylor & Francis)  
**DOI/URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13571516.2023.2168994  
**Publication Period:** 2023 (PRIMARY)

**Summary:** Finds significant negative short-run abnormal returns around cyber breach events and documents that negative CARs persist for up to 250 trading days post-announcement — substantially longer than most prior studies report. Industries with more frequent cyber attacks suffer larger per-incident losses, suggesting that sector-level cyber risk premium accumulates.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H1 (timing vs. firm characteristics):** Long-run persistence of losses implies that announcement-day timing alone does not capture total market cost; supports a longer event window
- **H4 (breach type/sector):** Industry-frequency interaction directly addresses sector-level heterogeneity
- **H5 (information asymmetry):** Persistent abnormal returns are consistent with slow resolution of information asymmetry rather than immediate price efficiency
- **Overall utility:** Strongest evidence for long-run market effects; counter to the "effects reverse within two weeks" finding from other studies

### Topic 5: News Pressure & Disclosure Timing — New

---

#### 9. Foerderer, J. & Schuetz, S. W. (2022)
**Title:** Data Breach Announcements and Stock Market Reactions: A Matter of Timing?  
**Journal:** *Management Science*, Vol. 68, No. 10, pp. 7298–7322 (INFORMS, 2022)  
**DOI/URL:** https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2021.4264  
**Publication Period:** 2022 (PRIMARY — within 5-year window)

**Summary:** Uses a large sample of U.S. breach announcements to show that firms strategically time disclosures to reduce market impact — announcing during high-news-pressure days (many competing stories) to bury coverage. Finds that higher news pressure on the announcement day significantly *weakens* negative market reactions. Firms disclose more severe breaches, internal-cause breaches, and healthcare/credential breaches more strategically. The paper provides causal evidence of disclosure timing as a market manipulation tool.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H1 (timing vs. firm characteristics):** Core paper for the "timing" component of H1; demonstrates that disclosure timing is endogenously chosen by firms, not exogenous — essential methodological caveat for any event study design
- **H2 (regulation impact):** Mandatory disclosure rules (FCC, SEC) constrain this strategic timing — one mechanism through which regulation should change market pricing
- **H4 (breach type):** Internal-cause and health/credential breaches are timed most strategically — confirms type-level heterogeneity
- **Overall utility:** **Must-cite** for H1 and as motivation for H2; establishes that pre-regulation timing endogeneity is a major confounder that post-regulation studies must address. Note: This was flagged by the Oct-12 monitor's AMCIS citation but not explicitly listed — adding now.
