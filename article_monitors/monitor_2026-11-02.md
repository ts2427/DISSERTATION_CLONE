### Topic 1: Regulatory Impact on Shareholder Value — Counterevidence and GDPR Evidence

---

#### 1. Ashraf, M. & Sunder, J. (2023)
**Title:** Can Shareholders Benefit from Consumer Protection Disclosure Mandates? Evidence from Data Breach Disclosure Laws  
**Journal:** *The Accounting Review*, Vol. 98, No. 4, pp. 1–32 (July 2023)  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.2308/TAR-2020-0787  
**Full text:** https://www.ebsco.com/articles/law/77368c72-268e-5b13-8f52-a04bbaa46e96/  
**Publication Period:** 2023 (PRIMARY — within 5-year window)

**Summary:** Uses the staggered passage of U.S. state data breach notification laws as a quasi-natural experiment to test shareholder wealth effects. Finds that cost of equity capital *decreases*, on average, after these laws take effect. Documents positive cumulative abnormal returns on key state-level legislative dates. Attributes the benefit to firms increasing cybersecurity investment and hiring dedicated cybersecurity officers after the laws. The risk-reduction effect is attenuated for firms that had already made real-options investments in cybersecurity before the law — suggesting prior adopters capture less incremental benefit.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H2 (FCC/SEC regulation impact on returns):** Core counterevidence to Obaydin et al. (2024) and Cao et al. (2024): this paper finds *positive* investor outcomes from mandatory disclosure laws, using the same staggered natural experiment design. Must be cited as the opposing empirical case; the difference likely turns on which outcome is measured (crash risk vs. cost of equity) and time horizon
- **H5 (volatility/information asymmetry):** Lower cost of equity implies reduced investor-perceived uncertainty — consistent with regulation narrowing the information asymmetry between firms and investors that H5 targets
- **H1 (firm characteristics):** The attenuation among prior cybersecurity investors is a firm-characteristic moderator — firms with higher baseline security posture respond differently to the mandate
- **Overall utility:** Published in one of the top three accounting journals; provides the necessary balancing citation for the crash-risk literature; creates a productive empirical debate framing for the dissertation's H2 section

#### 2. Motoki, F. & Pinto, J. (2025)
**Title:** Regulating data: Evidence from corporate America  
**Journal:** *Journal of Business Finance & Accounting*, Vol. 52, No. 1, pp. 541–568 (February 2025; first available online July 2024)  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/jbfa.12820  
**Full text:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jbfa.12820  
**Open Access:** UEA repository (ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/95934)  
**Publication Period:** 2025 (PRIMARY)

**Summary:** Uses GDPR enforcement on May 25, 2018 as a quasi-natural experiment on U.S. firms with European data exposure. Finds that GDPR-exposed firms lose 0.7–1.1% in market value relative to unexposed firms in the enforcement week. This is partly attributable to a decrease in sales growth among exposed firms. However, GDPR-exposed firms subsequently *increase* investment above control firms and become *less likely* to report a data breach post-regulation. Sample covers 1,013 U.S. firms around the week GDPR rules became enforceable. Provides a direct regulatory cost-benefit decomposition: short-term negative market reaction vs. long-term breach-risk reduction.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H2 (FCC/SEC regulation impact on returns):** Directly analogous empirical design: uses a major disclosure/privacy regulation's enforcement as the event, finds negative short-term market reaction — methodological template for testing the FCC's December 2023 rule
- **H5 (volatility/information asymmetry):** Post-GDPR firms are less likely to disclose breaches — this could reflect either genuine risk reduction (consistent with Ashraf & Sunder 2023) or strategic suppression (consistent with Obaydin et al. 2024); distinguishing between these interpretations is relevant to H5
- **H1 (firm characteristics in market pricing):** Heterogeneous effects across firms by degree of European data exposure — firm-level data geography as a moderating variable, analogous to H1's firm-characteristic heterogeneity
- **Overall utility:** Open-access JBFA paper; GDPR provides a cleaner regulatory shock than staggered state laws for causal identification; pair with Ashraf & Sunder (2023) and Cao et al. (2024) to establish the range of regulatory-event market-response estimates

### Topic 2: Options Markets, Informed Trading & Information Asymmetry

---

#### 3. Piccotti, L.R. & Wang, H. (2023)
**Title:** Informed trading in the options market surrounding data breaches  
**Journal:** *Global Finance Journal*, Vol. 56 (2023)  
**DOI/URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S104402832200076X  
**Publication Period:** 2023 (PRIMARY)

**Summary:** Studies directional options trading activity around 264 data breach announcements at U.S. public firms. Finds pervasive evidence of pre-disclosure informed trading in the options market: put-call ratios have predictive power for subsequent stock returns, consistent with investors exploiting private breach knowledge before public disclosure. The magnitude of the abnormal options activity is greatest for firms that have experienced *reoccurring* breaches, and for breaches deemed most severe. Reports significantly negative cumulative abnormal stock returns of −0.35% within one day of the announcement. Notes that increased regulatory scrutiny of breach disclosures has been associated with a decrease in the intensity of informed trading in more recent periods.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H5 (volatility/information asymmetry):** Options market put-call ratios document information asymmetry directly: some traders possess private breach information before mandatory public disclosure — a market microstructure manifestation of H5's information asymmetry costs
- **H3 (prior breach history penalty):** Greater informed trading activity around reoccurring breaches suggests markets are more uncertain about repeat offenders' undisclosed cyber risk — provides a mechanism for the elevated penalties documented by Peng & Zhang (2023)
- **H2 (regulation impact):** The observed decrease in informed trading during periods of heightened regulatory scrutiny supports the hypothesis that disclosure mandates can improve market information efficiency — a direct H2 mechanism
- **H4 (breach type effects):** Severity is a key moderator of informed trading intensity — consistent with more severe/sensitive breaches generating greater information asymmetry
- **Overall utility:** One of the few papers documenting the pre-disclosure informed trading channel that is the core mechanism behind H5; complements Chen et al. (2024) on insider selling and Obaydin et al. (2024) on crash risk; check if an updated version (the ResearchGate listing mentions a 283-breach sample with a post-COVID comparison) has been published
