### Topic 1: SEC/FCC Disclosure Rules — Market Reactions and Disclosure Quality

---

#### 1. Parupati, S. (2025)
**Title:** Mandatory Cybersecurity Disclosure: Early Evidence  
**Repository:** SSRN Working Paper No. 5268533  
**URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5268533  
**Publication Period:** 2025 (PRIMARY — within 5-year window)

**Summary:** Applies large language models to score the quality of cybersecurity disclosures in 10-K and 8-K filings under the SEC's December 2023 cybersecurity rule. Firms with higher disclosure-quality scores receive statistically positive market reactions, while low-quality disclosures generate insignificant or negative reactions. Higher disclosure quality also predicts narrower bid-ask spreads, lower analyst forecast errors, and reduced abnormal trading volume in the post-announcement window, indicating reduced information asymmetry.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H2 (FCC/SEC regulation impact on stock returns):** Provides early direct evidence on the SEC's 2023 rule's market effects; shows that *how much* a firm discloses determines the sign and magnitude of market reaction — a nuance critical for distinguishing "mandatory disclosure" (the regulation) from "high-quality disclosure" (firm compliance quality)
- **H5 (volatility/information asymmetry):** The bid-ask spread and abnormal trading volume results directly measure post-announcement information asymmetry; higher disclosure quality reduces information asymmetry costs for outside investors
- **H1 (firm characteristics in market pricing):** Disclosure quality is itself a firm characteristic that moderates the market reaction to breach announcements; this connects disclosure mandates to firm-level heterogeneity
- **Overall utility:** Key empirical complement to Murillo (2026) and the Block (2025) arXiv paper already in the literature; the LLM-scoring methodology may also be replicable for dissertation robustness tests

#### 2. Murillo, D. (2026)
**Title:** Beyond the Initial Filing: Cybersecurity Incident Disclosure Chains Under SEC Form 8-K Item 1.05  
**Repository:** SSRN Working Paper No. 7222059  
**URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7222059  
**Publication Period:** 2026 (PRIMARY)

**Summary:** Analyzes 53 multi-filing disclosure chains constructed from 78 SEC Item 1.05 (8-K) records filed December 2023 through July 2026. Demonstrates that standard event-study designs that use a single filing as the event date misattribute the information release, since most material incidents generate amendment chains with new material information in later filings. Argues that the standard approach produces both Type I and Type II errors in event-window returns.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H1 (timing vs. firm characteristics):** The disclosure-chain structure means "timing" is not a single event date but a sequence; H1 tests for disclosure delay may need to account for multi-filing events
- **H2 (FCC/SEC regulation):** Under the SEC's four-business-day rule, amendment chains complicate materiality assessment; the paper's methodology is a prerequisite for clean identification in any SEC-era event study
- **Overall utility:** Primarily a methodological contribution; the dissertation should cite this in the empirical design section to justify event-window and event-date choices; it also provides the most current census of Item 1.05 filings (78 through July 2026)

#### 3. [Authors TBD] (2024)
**Title:** Empirical Evidence on Disclosing Cyber Breaches in an 8-K Report: Initial Exploratory Evidence  
**Journal:** *Journal of Accounting and Public Policy* (2024)  
**URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0278425424000498  
**Publication Period:** 2024 (PRIMARY) — *Author names not confirmed in search results; retrieve full paper for complete citation and DOI*

**Summary:** Analyzes 670 publicly disclosed cyber breaches and compares market reactions across disclosure method (8-K filing versus other public channels). Firms that voluntarily chose 8-K as their disclosure vehicle show a significantly negative abnormal return at announcement but a positive, significant post-announcement drift, suggesting markets overcorrect at announcement. Firms using other disclosure methods show the opposite pattern — positive announcement-day returns followed by negative post-announcement drift. Interpreted as 8-K disclosure conveying credibility that reduces initial uncertainty but overcorrects the market.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H1 (timing vs. firm characteristics):** The *choice* of disclosure channel (8-K vs. other) is a firm-characteristic variable that predicts return patterns; under mandatory SEC rules, this cross-sectional variation shifts to *quality* of the mandated filing
- **H2 (FCC/SEC regulation):** Directly tests 8-K disclosure versus alternative channels; the post-announcement drift in 8-K firms suggests markets require time to fully process mandatory disclosures — consistent with temporary information asymmetry
- **H5 (volatility/information asymmetry):** The announcement-day overcorrection and subsequent drift is a classic information asymmetry pattern — investors face high uncertainty at the event date and revise beliefs as more information arrives
- **Overall utility:** Confirm full citation from ScienceDirect before citing; the contrast between 8-K and non-8-K disclosers is the cleanest natural experiment on disclosure *channel* (not just disclosure *timing*) in the recent literature

### Topic 2: Disclosure Timing Trade-Offs

---

#### 4. Ashraf, M., Jiang, J. (Xuefeng) & Wang, I.Y. (2022/2023)
**Title:** Are There Trade-Offs with Mandating Timely Disclosure of Cybersecurity Incidents? Evidence from State-Level Data Breach Disclosure Laws  
**Repository:** SSRN Working Paper No. 4068575  
**URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4068575  
**Publication Period:** 2022 (working paper; check for journal version) — *Note: distinct from "Ashraf & Sunder (2023)" already in prior monitors*

**Summary:** Uses staggered adoption of U.S. state data breach notification laws to study the speed-detail tradeoff in mandated disclosure. Finds that when facing a statutory deadline, firms disclose breaches 90% faster — but are 58% less likely to include breach details. Despite less detail, investors still respond *negatively* to delayed disclosures, but are significantly more forgiving of timing delay when the delay is associated with higher subsequent detail quality. Introduces the concept of a "disclosure quality–timing frontier" under regulatory mandates.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H1 (timing vs. firm characteristics in market pricing):** Timing is not a pure firm characteristic but a regulatory artifact; the tradeoff between speed and detail content means disclosure timing is endogenous to both regulatory regime and managerial strategy — key for identifying H1's causal mechanism
- **H2 (FCC/SEC regulation impact):** FCC and SEC mandates impose timelines (seven business days under FCC; four business days under SEC Item 1.05) that may systematically reduce disclosure detail quality; this paper provides the empirical basis for predicting that regulation may *worsen* disclosure quality even as it accelerates timing
- **H5 (information asymmetry):** The speed-detail frontier predicts that early, low-detail disclosures create residual information asymmetry — markets receive a signal but lack the content to price it fully, generating elevated volatility and trading volume
- **Overall utility:** This is a different paper from "Ashraf & Sunder (2023)" — verify that both are in the literature review; the tradeoff finding is the strongest challenge to simple "mandatory disclosure → better markets" arguments and is essential for H2's discussion of regulatory costs

### Topic 3: Breach Type Heterogeneity

---

#### 5. Rasoulian, S., Grégoire, Y., Legoux, R. & Sénécal, S. (2023)
**Title:** The Effects of Service Crises and Recovery Resources on Market Reactions: An Event Study Analysis on Data Breach Announcements  
**Journal:** *Journal of Service Research*, Vol. 26, No. 4 (2023; published online 2021)  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/10946705211036944  
**URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10946705211036944  
**Publication Period:** 2023 (PRIMARY — journal publication); 2021 (online first — flag as borderline)

**Summary:** Event study of 217 data breach announcements that classifies events by *type of breached data* (financial, Social Security numbers, medical information) and *breach procedure* (external hacker attack, equipment theft, insider). Finds that the type of breached data and breach procedure each independently moderate cumulative abnormal returns. Introduces a service-crisis framing: breach announcements are a service failure event, and firms with pre-existing crisis recovery resources (strong customer trust, service infrastructure) experience smaller market penalties than firms that lack these buffers.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H4 (breach type effects on market reaction):** This is the most direct recent empirical test of H4; the paper explicitly tests financial versus medical versus SSN breaches and finds differential market reactions by data type. The classification framework maps onto H4's design
- **H1 (firm characteristics):** Pre-existing service recovery resources are firm characteristics that moderate the market reaction — an underexplored moderator alongside firm size, industry, and governance
- **H3 (prior breach history):** Firms that have previously experienced breaches likely have depleted recovery resources — this paper's framing connects the repeat-breach penalty to the depletion of reputational/service buffers
- **Overall utility:** Strong peer-reviewed citation for H4 in a marketing/strategy outlet (*Journal of Service Research* is FT50-adjacent); the data type classification (financial/SSN/medical) is directly adoptable as an H4 coding framework. Flag 2021 online publication date alongside 2023 formal publication

#### 6. Zhu, Y. (2025)
**Title:** Data Breach Notification Laws and Corporate Tax Planning  
**Repository:** SSRN Working Paper No. 5297180  
**URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5297180  
**Publication Period:** 2025 (PRIMARY)

**Summary:** Uses staggered state-level adoption of data breach notification laws to identify the effect on corporate tax avoidance. Finds a statistically significant 3.8% decrease in the long-run effective tax rate following law adoption, suggesting that mandatory disclosure obligations prompt firms to shift resources toward transparency and compliance and away from aggressive tax strategies. The tax-planning channel is distinct from stock-return or market-liquidity effects studied in earlier work.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H2 (FCC/SEC regulation impact):** Part of the broader regulatory-impact literature; demonstrates that breach notification laws have diffuse corporate governance effects beyond market returns — useful for framing H2 as one facet of a wider regulatory shock
- **Overall utility:** Less direct than primary H2 papers, but useful for the literature review section on "regulatory consequences of mandatory disclosure" to show that breach notification laws affect multiple dimensions of firm behavior simultaneously; cite alongside Wei & Zhu (payout policy) and Zhu, Zhou & Gao (accounting conservatism)

#### 7. Wei, Z. & Zhu, Y.
**Title:** Data Breach Notification Laws and Corporate Payout Policy  
**Repository:** SSRN Working Paper No. 4715249  
**URL:** https://ssrn.com/abstract=4715249  
**Publication Period:** 2024 (working paper) — *Publication year not confirmed from search results; verify on SSRN page*

**Summary:** Examines whether the adoption of state data breach notification laws alters corporate payout decisions. Finds that affected firms reduce cash dividend payments while simultaneously increasing share repurchases following law adoption. The authors interpret this as managers trading the commitment of dividends (harder to cut) for the flexibility of buybacks under heightened disclosure and litigation uncertainty. The result is consistent with regulatory uncertainty increasing financial flexibility demands.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H2 (FCC/SEC regulation impact on market behavior):** Demonstrates that disclosure mandates alter corporate financial policy in ways that could secondarily affect stock returns through capital structure and payout signaling — a confound to control for in H2 empirical tests
- **Overall utility:** Cite in the regulatory-impact framing alongside Zhu (2025) tax paper and accounting conservatism paper; together these three papers document that breach notification laws have pervasive corporate-governance effects beyond the direct market-reaction channel
