### Article N91 — NEW ⭐⭐⭐ [2025 PUBLICATION]
**Martins, A.M., Moutinho, N. & Cró, S. (2025).** Stock market effects of major cyber-attacks: evidence for breached and cybersecurity listed firms. *Journal of Banking Regulation*.
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1057/s41261-025-00293-y
**Springer URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41261-025-00293-y
**ResearchGate:** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395455081_Stock_market_effects_of_major_cyber-attacks_evidence_for_breached_and_cybersecurity_listed_firms
**Published:** Accepted August 28, 2025; published online September 2025 (Springer Nature)

**Summary:** Martins, Moutinho, and Cró conduct an event study covering 53 global cyberattacks and find a negative and statistically significant stock price reaction for breached firms around the disclosure date. Smaller firms, firms with higher leverage, higher growth opportunities, and those suffering financial information loss exhibit more severe market reactions. Crucially, cybersecurity firms listed on public markets experience *positive* and statistically significant abnormal returns following peer-firm breach disclosures, suggesting a spillover benefit channel where competitor incidents drive demand for security products.

**Relevance Analysis:**
- **H1 (Firm characteristics → market pricing):** ⭐⭐⭐ The cross-sectional findings (size, leverage, growth) directly operationalize H1's firm characteristics moderators in an international event study. Smaller firms and higher-leverage firms face more severe negative returns — consistent with H1's prediction that firm financial vulnerability amplifies market reaction magnitude.
- **H4 (Breach type — financial information):** ⭐⭐⭐ Financial information loss is singled out as a significant negative-return moderator within the breach-type dimension, providing direct H4 support. This aligns with the earlier literature finding that PII and financial data breaches generate larger abnormal returns than system disruption events.
- **H5 (Volatility / information asymmetry):** ⭐⭐ The cybersecurity firm spillover finding suggests information contagion across the sector — market participants update beliefs about unannounced firm-level vulnerabilities when a competitor is breached, a mechanism consistent with the information asymmetry widening that H5 predicts.
- **H2 (Regulation):** The international dataset (53 global attacks) provides cross-jurisdictional variation in disclosure timing and regulatory regimes — a useful comparative backdrop for H2's FCC/SEC U.S. federal mandate analysis.
- **FLAG:** ⭐⭐⭐ *Journal of Banking Regulation (Springer Nature / Palgrave) — peer-reviewed finance/regulation journal. Published September 2025. Authors: António Miguel Martins, Nuno Moutinho, Susana Cró (Portuguese university affiliations). DOI confirmed (10.1057/s41261-025-00293-y). - **H4 (Extreme breach type):** The focus on extreme events creates a breach-severity moderator — high-severity breaches generate disproportionately larger long-term valuation losses than moderate events, consistent with H4's type/severity dimension.
- **H1 (Firm characteristics):** Firms that have invested in visible cybersecurity governance and disclosure quality should exhibit lower market intolerance effects, linking H1's governance variable to the escalating intolerance mechanism.

## KEY NEW INSIGHTS THIS CYCLE

### The "Escalating Market Intolerance" Finding — H3 Critical Update

Ryan, Withers & den Hartog (N93) document that the market's tolerance for cyberattack events is declining over time — each successive breach triggers progressively more severe valuation losses. This finding, combined with ISS STOXX's 250-day sustained underperformance data (N94), creates a strong theoretical and empirical foundation for H3:

- **H3 mechanism established:** The market penalizes repeat breaches not merely because it aggregates direct costs (N93's "escalating intolerance" narrative) but because each repeat event revises upward the market's assessment of the firm's persistent cybersecurity risk posture — a reputational capital depletion model.
- **H3 quantification available:** N94 provides the 250-day trajectory (-4.9% average, -8.5% Finance, -8.3% Healthcare) as H3's long-horizon benchmark. A repeat-breach firm at 250 days can be expected to show worse underperformance relative to this baseline, testable through the difference-in-differences design.

### U-Shaped Firm-Size Effect — H1 Non-Linear Prediction

Baksy, Caratelli & Olson (N95) find a U-shaped vulnerability curve by firm size (mid-size firms most vulnerable). This is the most novel H1 development this cycle:
- The standard H1 prediction (linear negative relationship between firm size and breach market impact) may be misspecified.
- Mid-size firms may face *worse* breach market reactions than the linear model predicts, due to both higher actual cybersecurity risk exposure (N95) and lower analyst/investor capacity to assess that risk (information asymmetry premium for mid-size firms).
- H1's empirical model should consider a quadratic or spline specification for the size term.

### Confirmed: Dual Cybersecurity Firm Spillover — Novel H4/H5 Channel

Martins, Moutinho & Cró (N91) find that cybersecurity sector firms earn *positive* abnormal returns when peer firms are breached. This spillover effect (breached firm losses → cybersecurity vendor gains) confirms:
- **H4 channel extension:** Breach type determines not just the magnitude of breached-firm losses but the cross-sector information spillover pattern.
- **H5 extension:** Information asymmetry reductions at the portfolio level (negative spillover for broad market) may be partially offset by positive information externalities in the cybersecurity sector.
