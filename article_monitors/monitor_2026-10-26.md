### Topic 1: Mandatory Disclosure Laws — Unintended Financial Market Consequences

#### 1. Chen, X., Hilary, G. & Tian, X.S. (2024/2025)
**Title:** Mandatory Data Breach Disclosure and Insider Trading  
**Journal:** *Journal of Business Finance & Accounting* (Wiley, e-pub December 2024)  
**DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1111/jbfa.12842  
**Full text:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jbfa.12842  
**Publication Period:** 2024/2025 (PRIMARY)

**Summary:** Studies whether state-level data breach disclosure mandates affect insider trading profits. Finds that insiders earn *greater* abnormal selling profits after a state adopts a breach notification law, suggesting insiders exploit the pre-disclosure window before mandatory public notification. The effect is concentrated at firms with weaker governance and higher cyber risk. Stricter laws (shorter notification windows) partially reduce, but do not eliminate, the insider trading advantage.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H2 (FCC/SEC regulation impact on returns):** Core finding: regulation designed to improve market transparency generates an unintended insider-trading externality — insiders profit more from information asymmetry, not less; challenges a naive "regulation = better pricing" prediction
- **H5 (volatility/information asymmetry):** Information asymmetry between insiders and public investors is *widened* in the window between breach discovery and mandatory public disclosure
- **H6 (governance response):** Weaker governance amplifies the insider trading response — governance quality is a key moderating variable
- **Overall utility:** One of the strongest 2024 papers on H2 mechanism; pair with Obaydin et al. (below) to show two distinct channels by which notification laws distort information markets

#### 2. Obaydin, I., Xu, L. & Zurbruegg, R. (2024)
**Title:** The unintended cost of data breach notification laws: Evidence from managerial bad news hoarding  
**Journal:** *Journal of Business Finance & Accounting*, Vol. 51, Issues 9–10, pp. 2709–2736  
**DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1111/jbfa.12794  
**Full text:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jbfa.12794  
**Open access:** Available via University of Adelaide repository; CC BY license  
**Publication Period:** 2024 (PRIMARY)

**Summary:** Uses staggered adoption of U.S. state breach notification laws as a natural experiment. Finds that adopting mandatory notification *raises* future stock price crash risk. Interprets this as managers stockpiling negative information — withholding bad news until forced disclosure triggers a clustered revelation, causing sudden price crashes. Effect is stronger when managers have greater ability or incentive to hoard information.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H2 (regulation impact):** Mandatory disclosure (notification threshold trigger) creates perverse incentives to withhold news below the threshold, concentrating negative information release — a second unintended consequence alongside Chen et al.
- **H5 (volatility/information asymmetry):** Crash risk is a direct measure of extreme negative return variance — exactly the volatility/asymmetry outcome H5 targets; this paper provides causal evidence using a natural experiment
- **H3 (prior breach history):** Firms with prior breaches may have learned to time disclosures near thresholds, amplifying the hoarding pattern
- **Overall utility:** Open-access natural experiment; strongest causal identification of H5 mechanism; must-cite alongside Cao et al. (2024) which uses the same empirical strategy

#### 3. Cao, H., Phan, H.V. & Silveri, S. (2024)
**Title:** Data breach disclosures and stock price crash risk: Evidence from data breach notification laws  
**Journal:** *International Review of Financial Analysis*, Vol. 93  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.irfa.2024.103164  
**Full text:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057521924000966  
**Publication Period:** 2024 (PRIMARY)

**Summary:** Exploits the staggered passage of U.S. state data breach notification laws (2002–2018) to show that adopting these laws is associated with *higher* future stock price crash risk. The effect is stronger for firms with weaker corporate governance, tighter financing constraints, and greater information asymmetry. Authors attribute this to increased investor worry about breach consequences combined with managerial incentives to suppress adverse information until mandated disclosure.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H2 (regulation impact on returns):** Direct empirical test of notification law effects on firm-level market outcomes; negative side-effect confirms regulation does not straightforwardly reduce pricing uncertainty
- **H5 (volatility/information asymmetry):** Crash risk is the key outcome variable; information asymmetry is both a moderator and a mechanism — exactly the H5 framework
- **H1 (firm characteristics in market pricing):** Governance quality and financing constraints are identified moderators — supports the heterogeneity predicted in H1
- **Overall utility:** Published in a top finance journal; natural-experiment design provides strong causal claims; use alongside Obaydin et al. (2024) as the twin causal papers on notification law effects

#### 4. Obaydin, I., Zurbruegg, R., Mihaylov, G. & Truong, C. (2024)
**Title:** Mandatory data breach disclosure laws and stock liquidity: The role of cybersecurity risk and reporting opacity  
**Repository:** SSRN Working Paper  
**URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7233704  
**Publication Period:** 2024 (working paper; PRIMARY)

**Summary:** Finds that state-level breach notification laws reduce relative and effective bid-ask spreads by approximately 7.5–8.3% of sample means, implying improved liquidity on average. However, the liquidity benefit is *concentrated at firms with low ex-ante cybersecurity risk*; firms with high cybersecurity risk see no significant improvement. Reporting opacity further moderates the law-liquidity relationship.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H5 (information asymmetry/volatility):** Bid-ask spread reduction is the canonical measure of information asymmetry in market microstructure; this paper directly tests whether mandatory disclosure reduces H5-type costs — answer: only for low-risk firms
- **H2 (regulation impact):** Heterogeneous liquidity effect means FCC/SEC-style regulation will not uniformly improve market quality; high-risk firms (likely telecom, healthcare) may see no benefit
- **H1 (firm characteristics):** Cybersecurity risk level and reporting opacity are the firm-level moderators — supports cross-sectional heterogeneity in regulatory impact
- **Overall utility:** Working paper so cite with caveat; provides a market microstructure extension of H5 not covered by crash-risk papers; note: same lead author (Obaydin) has the JBFA bad-news-hoarding paper above — citable as a multi-paper programme

#### 5. Peng, J. & Zhang, H. (2023)
**Title:** Repeated Data Breaches and Firm Value  
**Journal:** *Economics Letters* (Elsevier, 2023)  
**URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165176523000265  
**Publication Period:** 2023 (PRIMARY — within 5-year window)  
**Note:** Confirm DOI via ScienceDirect; pii S0165176523000265 corresponds to *Econ Letters*

**Summary:** Studies 644 U.S. publicly listed firm breaches from 2004 to 2021. Finds that the *second* breach hurts firm value more than the first, but third-and-subsequent breaches show no further incremental reduction. Replacing the CEO after a first breach limits the market penalty on subsequent breaches. Financial information breaches (e.g., credit card data) generate the most severe value destruction.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H3 (prior breach history penalty):** Core paper for H3 — establishes non-linear penalty structure: second breach worst, then plateau; CEO replacement as a mitigating factor is a testable governance interaction
- **H4 (breach type effects):** Financial data breach type is more damaging than other categories — supports the type-differentiation prediction in H4
- **H6 (executive turnover):** CEO replacement after first breach is empirically linked to lower penalties on future breaches — provides the governance-response mechanism for H6
- **Overall utility:** Short-sample Letters paper but with a clean design; must-pair with the follow-up Zhang et al. (2025) below

#### 6. Zhang, P., Peng, J., Mao, X. & Xu, Y. (2025)
**Title:** Repeated data breaches and executive compensation  
**Journal:** *Applied Economics Letters*, Vol. 32, No. 8, pp. 1111–1120 (Taylor & Francis, 2025)  
**URL:** https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/apeclt/v32y2025i8p1111-1120.html  
**Publication Period:** 2025 (PRIMARY)

**Summary:** Companion paper to Peng & Zhang (2023). Analyzes how firms adjust executive pay structures after experiencing repeated breaches. Finds that total CEO compensation *falls* after repeated breaches, consistent with increased board scrutiny, but non-CEO executive pay moves in the opposite direction. Interprets this as boards holding CEOs accountable for cybersecurity failures.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H6 (executive turnover/governance response):** Compensation cuts are a governance disciplining mechanism short of full turnover — demonstrates that boards respond to repeat breaches even when the CEO stays
- **H3 (prior breach history):** Same dataset as Peng & Zhang (2023); repeated breach experience triggers governance adjustments that could moderate market reactions
- **Overall utility:** Extends the Peng & Zhang programme into compensation; together the two papers provide CEO turnover *and* compensation as governance channels for H6

#### 7. Charoen, D. & Khern-am-nuai, W. (2024)
**Title:** Revisiting the (disappearing) cost of data breach disclosures  
**Journal:** *Digital Policy, Regulation and Governance*, Vol. 27, No. 1, pp. 37–52 (Emerald, 2024)  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1108/DPRG-02-2024-0033  
**Publication Period:** 2024 (PRIMARY)

**Summary:** Event study of S&P 500 data breach disclosures from 2013 to 2021 using Fama-French three-factor model. Finds that negative market reactions to breach announcements have become *significantly weaker* in recent years compared to the prior decade. Attributes the decline to shifting consumer perceptions that normalize breach events — breach "market fatigue" as a demand-side phenomenon rather than a supply-side governance improvement.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H1 (timing vs. firm characteristics in market pricing):** Documents a time trend in base-level market sensitivity — any post-regulation study must control for this secular attenuation
- **H2 (regulation impact):** Complicates identification: weakening reactions may reflect regulatory improvements OR consumer normalization; disentangling these mechanisms is a key challenge for H2 analysis
- **H3 (prior breach history):** If market sensitivity is falling overall, repeat-breach penalties may also attenuate over time — temporal interaction
- **Overall utility:** Directly motivates the need for a post-FCC/SEC regulation study to separate regulatory effects from secular trend effects

#### 8. Liu, C. & Babar, M.A. (2024; print 2026)
**Title:** Corporate cybersecurity risk and data breaches: A systematic review of empirical research  
**Journal:** *Australian Journal of Management*, Vol. 51, No. 1, pp. 62–92 (SAGE, online November 2024, print February 2026)  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/03128962241293658  
**Publication Period:** 2024/2026 (PRIMARY)

**Summary:** Systematic review of 203 empirical studies across 12 disciplines. Synthesizes evidence on: determinants of corporate cyber risk; market and operational consequences of breaches; whether executive turnover (CEO/CIO) after a breach reduces future risk (evidence is inconclusive — some studies find reduced risk, others find no change); and whether firms learn from breaches (Peng et al. 2023 finding that CEO replacement dampens repeat-breach penalties is cited as the clearest learning mechanism). Proposes an integrated framework linking breach drivers, impacts, and firm adaptation.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H6 (executive turnover/governance response):** Systematic synthesis of CIO/CEO turnover-post-breach literature; confirms that while replacement is common, its risk-reducing effect is unconfirmed — critical caveat for H6 causal claims
- **H3 (prior breach history):** Review covers the "firm learning" literature that H3 implicitly requires for interpretation
- **H4 (breach type):** Cross-study synthesis of breach type effects available
- **H5 (information asymmetry):** Review covers market reaction literature that underpins H5
- **Overall utility:** Best available comprehensive literature map for the dissertation; use as the primary literature review anchor for Chapters 1–2

#### 9. Authors TBD (2025)
**Title:** From breach to recovery: a survival analysis of stock market responses to data breaches  
**Journal:** *Information & Computer Security* (Emerald, 2025)  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1108/ICS-10-2025-0424  
**Full text:** https://www.emerald.com/ics/article-abstract/34/5/903/1393614/  
**Publication Period:** 2025 (PRIMARY — author names unconfirmed; retrieve full paper)

**Summary:** Event study of 3,316 U.S. publicly listed firm data breaches from 2006 to 2024, combined with survival analysis of return recovery. Finds a five-day abnormal return of approximately −0.26%, which dissipates within one month for most firms. Healthcare, retail, and telecommunications recover more slowly (7–10+ trading days), while durable goods and manufacturing show resilience. Firm size and R&D intensity are identified as determinants of recovery speed. Breaches involving sensitive financial or health data prolong recovery.

**Relevance to Hypotheses:**
- **H4 (breach type effects):** Sector heterogeneity and data type effects are explicitly tested in the largest recent U.S. sample — strong empirical support for H4
- **H1 (firm characteristics):** Firm size and R&D intensity as recovery determinants directly test the firm-level heterogeneity in H1
- **H5 (information asymmetry):** Recovery time maps onto duration of elevated information asymmetry — survival analysis framework is a novel operationalization of H5
- **Overall utility:** Largest recent event-study sample (3,316 events, 2006–2024); Emerald peer-reviewed; retrieve full paper for author attribution before citing

## FCC Regulatory Update

The FCC's 2024 data breach notification rule for telecommunications carriers (7-business-day notification to authorities; 30-day customer notification for 500+ affected customers; expanded PII scope beyond CPNI) was upheld by the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals on August 13–14, 2025. Industry groups may appeal to the Supreme Court. No academic market-reaction study of the FCC rule's financial market effects was identified this week — this remains a gap in the literature that the dissertation could address.
