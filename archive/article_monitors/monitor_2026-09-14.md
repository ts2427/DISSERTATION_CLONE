### Article N82 — NEW ⭐⭐⭐ [2026 PUBLICATION]
**Viancourt, S. & Walkup, B.R. (2026).** Data breach announcements: evaluating the content and timing of breach announcements and their effect on firm value. *Information and Computer Security*, 34(1), 170–186.
**Emerald URL:** https://www.emerald.com/ics/article/34/1/170/1298013/Data-breach-announcements-evaluating-the-content
**AISNET preprint:** https://aisel.aisnet.org/sais2023/14/
**Rollins College DBA Repository:** https://scholarship.rollins.edu/dba_dissertations/38/
**⚠️ Note:** DOI not confirmed from search results — verify via Emerald Insight institutional access. Published as Vol. 34, No. 1, 2026 per Emerald catalog entry.

**Summary:** Viancourt and Walkup conduct an event study of publicly disclosed data breach announcements using an event study methodology followed by regression analysis. The paper identifies negative abnormal returns in two short-term event windows, with preannouncement data leakage detected in the (−3,3) window. Critically, periods of high market volatility caused abnormal returns to be significantly more negative, while firm-specific post-breach activities (remediation actions, public statements) did not mitigate negative abnormal returns — suggesting that what the firm does after the breach matters less to the market than the broader environment at the time of disclosure.

**Relevance Analysis:**
- **H1 (Firm characteristics / disclosure timing):** ⭐⭐⭐ The preannouncement leakage finding in the (−3,3) window directly supports H1's timing dimension: markets price breach information before the official disclosure date, implying that disclosure timing relative to information leakage determines market pricing. The market volatility moderator (high volatility → more negative returns) is a novel systemic moderator for H1 that the prior literature has not prominently tested — this suggests macroeconomic conditions at the time of disclosure interact with breach characteristics.
- **H5 (Information asymmetry / volatility):** ⭐⭐⭐ Preannouncement data leakage is the classic H5 event-study marker — information asymmetry between informed and uninformed investors generates abnormal returns before the public announcement. The finding that high market volatility amplifies negative returns may reflect that elevated VIX environments cause investors to assign greater uncertainty to breach consequences (widening the information asymmetry cost), consistent with H5's volatility dimension.
- **H2 (Regulation):** Mandatory disclosure (FCC/SEC) is designed precisely to prevent preannouncement leakage by standardizing the notification timeline — if mandatory disclosure rules reduce the (−3,3) pre-announcement window abnormal returns, this is a direct H2 measurable outcome. The pre/post-regulation comparison of leakage windows is a novel H2 empirical design.
- **H4 (Breach type):** The regression analysis of attack vectors provides a breach-type moderator within a single event study — different attack vectors (hacking vs. insider threats vs. accidental exposure) may generate differential returns. This is directly applicable to H4.
- **H3 (Repeat-breach penalty):** Firm-specific post-breach activities failing to mitigate returns implies the market penalizes the breach itself rather than the firm's response — for H3, this means the reputational penalty from repeat breaches is not remediated by improved disclosure quality post-breach.
- **FLAG:** ⭐⭐⭐ *Information and Computer Security (Emerald) — peer-reviewed IS/security journal. 2026 publication (Vol. 34, No. 1). Authors: Viancourt and Walkup. The preannouncement leakage finding and volatility moderator are novel H1/H5 contributions. The finding that post-breach firm actions don't mitigate returns is important for H3's reputational persistence argument. Verify DOI via institutional access.*

### Article N83 — NEW ⭐⭐⭐ [2025 PUBLICATION]
**Chen, X., Hilary, G. & Tian, X. (2025).** Mandatory data breach disclosure and insider trading. *Journal of Business Finance & Accounting*, 52(5), 2091–2110.
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/jbfa.12842
**Wiley URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jbfa.12842
**SSRN:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5012929
**Bristol Research:** https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/mandatory-data-breach-disclosure-and-insider-trading
**Published:** November 2025 (Vol. 52, Issue 5)

**Summary:** Chen, Hilary, and Tian examine whether mandatory data breach disclosure laws affect insider-selling behavior, finding that selling profits are significantly greater after states adopt mandatory disclosure requirements. The effect is driven by insider exploitation of the period between breach discovery and mandatory public disclosure — insiders sell before the mandated disclosure and profit from the information advantage. The excess insider profits are more pronounced for firms with weaker corporate governance, higher cybersecurity risk, and without prior investment in material cyber protection programs. Insiders profit less in states with stricter laws and where litigation risk is elevated.

**Relevance Analysis:**
- **H2 (FCC/SEC regulation → stock returns):** ⭐⭐⭐ This paper's central finding is that mandatory disclosure laws create a new form of information asymmetry exploitation — insiders trade on breach information between discovery and disclosure. This is the adverse selection problem that mandatory disclosure is supposed to solve, but Chen et al. show it instead creates a strategic trading window. For H2, this means that mandatory disclosure rules (FCC 7-day, SEC 4-day) should compress the insider trading window, reducing pre-disclosure abnormal returns and insider trading profits. The FCC/SEC rules are significantly faster than state-level notification laws — the dissertation should test whether faster federal mandates (vs. longer state mandates) reduce the insider trading window more effectively.
- **H5 (Information asymmetry):** ⭐⭐⭐ Insider trading profits are the most direct measure of information asymmetry costs — Chen et al. provide empirical evidence that mandatory disclosure laws, by creating a predictable disclosure date, actually increase (not decrease) information asymmetry by enabling insiders to front-run the disclosure. This is a fundamental H5 mechanism that the prior literature's event-study approach missed entirely: the asymmetry is between insiders who know both the breach and the disclosure date vs. outside investors who know neither.
- **H1 (Firm characteristics):** Firms with weaker governance, higher cybersecurity risk, and without prior cyber protection investment show larger insider trading profits — these are precisely the H1 firm-level moderators (governance quality, cyber posture, firm size as a proxy for governance strength) that predict cross-sectional variation in market pricing.
- **H3 (Prior breach history):** Firms with prior breach history may have better-defined disclosure protocols (knowing the disclosure process from experience), potentially reducing the insider trading window for repeat-breach firms — a novel inverse H3 mechanism where prior experience reduces information asymmetry exploitation.
- **H6 (Executive turnover):** If insiders exploit the disclosure window, governance responses (board oversight, audit committee involvement, CISO escalation procedures) that shorten the discovery-to-disclosure interval would reduce both insider trading profits and motivate subsequent executive turnover as governance failures are revealed. H6 and H5 interact through the insider trading channel.
- **FLAG:** ⭐⭐⭐ *Journal of Business Finance & Accounting (Wiley) — top-ranked accounting/finance journal. Published November 2025, Vol. 52(5). Authors: Xi Chen, Gilles Hilary, Xiaoli (Shaolee) Tian. DOI confirmed (10.1111/jbfa.12842). This is a priority citation for H2 and H5: it provides the strongest existing evidence that mandatory disclosure laws generate strategic information asymmetry exploitation, and the FCC/SEC faster mandates should compress this window further. Essential for the information asymmetry section of H5 and the regulatory mechanism section of H2.*

### Article N84 — NEW ⭐⭐⭐ [2024 PUBLICATION — FLAGGED AS HIGHLY RELEVANT]
**Obaydin, I., Xu, R. & Zurbruegg, R. (2024).** The unintended cost of data breach notification laws: Evidence from managerial bad news hoarding. *Journal of Business Finance & Accounting*, 51(9–10), 2709–2736.
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/jbfa.12794
**Wiley URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jbfa.12794
**Semantic Scholar:** https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-Unintended-Cost-of-Data-Breach-Notification-Bad-Obaydin-Xu/04b213500970eb7614e62aee08ed818ee1662af0
**Published:** 2024 (Vol. 51, Issues 9–10) ⚠️ *Flagged: 2024 publication, slightly outside primary 2021–2026 window but highly relevant*

**Summary:** Obaydin, Xu, and Zurbruegg examine the staggered adoption of state-level data breach notification laws and find that mandatory disclosure requirements increase the risk of stock price crashes. The paradoxical mechanism is that improved cybersecurity disclosure transparency incentivizes managers to hoard *other* negative information (unrelated to breaches) to prevent market overreactions at the disclosure event — managers bundle bad news timing strategically around the breach disclosure obligation. The result is that consumer protection disclosure laws have unintended consequences for non-targeted stakeholders: investors face increased crash risk due to bad news accumulation, even as breach-specific disclosure transparency improves.

**Relevance Analysis:**
- **H2 (FCC/SEC regulation → stock returns):** ⭐⭐⭐ The unintended crash risk finding is a critical counterpoint to H2's prediction that mandatory disclosure reduces information asymmetry and stabilizes returns. If mandatory disclosure triggers bad news hoarding in *non-breach* information, the net effect on stock return stability is ambiguous — mandatory disclosure reduces breach-specific information asymmetry but increases crash risk from accumulated non-breach bad news. H2 should explicitly address this unintended consequence finding and argue that the FCC's focused, incident-level disclosure (7-day notification) is less susceptible to triggering broad bad-news hoarding than the state-level laws Obaydin et al. study (which are disclosure *events* that managers plan around strategically).
- **H5 (Information asymmetry / volatility):** ⭐⭐⭐ Stock price crash risk is the canonical measure of accumulated information asymmetry — crash risk spikes when previously hoarded information is suddenly released. The finding that disclosure laws increase crash risk is H5-critical: mandatory breach disclosure, by creating predictable negative news events, may inadvertently increase volatility and crash risk beyond the breach-specific window. This is the most direct evidence that regulatory disclosure mandates have systematic volatility consequences (H5) beyond the announcement-day effect.
- **H3 (Prior breach history):** Firms with prior breach history have already established disclosure protocols and investor expectations — they may hoard less other bad news around subsequent breach disclosures (because investors already expect bad news from them). This predicts a smaller incremental crash risk increase for repeat-breach firms under mandatory disclosure laws — a testable H3 moderator.
- **H1 (Firm characteristics):** Agency problems (proxied by governance weakness, managerial discretion) predict which firms engage in bad-news hoarding — directly mapping onto H1's firm-level governance moderators.
- **FLAG:** ⭐⭐⭐ *Journal of Business Finance & Accounting (Wiley) — top-ranked accounting/finance journal. Published 2024, Vol. 51(9–10). Authors: Ivan Obaydin, Rui Xu, Ralf Zurbruegg (University of Adelaide). DOI confirmed (10.1111/jbfa.12794). FLAGGED 2024 — highly relevant despite being outside primary window. Together with N83 (Chen et al., 2025), N84 establishes that mandatory disclosure laws have multiple unintended information asymmetry consequences (insider trading exploitation + bad-news hoarding + crash risk). These two JBFA papers represent a critical cluster for H2's "unintended consequences of mandatory disclosure" discussion.*

**Summary:** Zhang, Zhang, and Jiang examine how breached firms manage their "legitimacy gap" — the reputational damage caused by a breach relative to stakeholder expectations — through strategic forward-looking disclosure. **Relevance Analysis:**
- **H3 (Prior breach history / repeat-breach penalty):** ⭐⭐⭐ Recurring breaches are associated with *more* forward-looking narratives — breached firms that have suffered repeated events engage in more aggressive legitimacy repair communication. - **H2 (Regulation):** Post–disclosure law breaches are associated with more forward-looking narratives — firms facing mandatory disclosure obligations (which increase the market's expectation of informative disclosure) respond by providing more optimistic forward-looking content to counterbalance the negative mandated disclosure. This is a novel H2 interaction: mandatory disclosure rules may inadvertently increase strategic communication around breach events.
- **H5 (Information asymmetry):** Forward-looking narratives without forecast revisions create a new form of soft information asymmetry: managers appear more optimistic than their private earnings forecasts suggest. This "cheap talk" legitimacy strategy may temporarily reduce perceived information asymmetry (investors see optimistic narrative) while not resolving real information asymmetry (earnings forecasts unchanged).

## KEY NEW INSIGHTS THIS CYCLE

### The "Mandatory Disclosure Unintended Consequences" Cluster — H2 Critical Update

Three papers this week form a critical cluster documenting that mandatory breach disclosure laws produce multiple *unintended* information asymmetry consequences:

1. **N83 (Chen, Hilary & Tian, 2025):** Mandatory disclosure laws create a strategic insider trading window — insiders exploit the discovery-to-disclosure interval for abnormal selling profits.
2. **N84 (Obaydin, Xu & Zurbruegg, 2024):** Mandatory disclosure laws increase stock price crash risk — managers hoard non-breach bad news to prevent overreactions at mandatory disclosure events.
3. **N88 (Agarwal, Agarwal & Chatterjee, 2024):** Mandatory disclosure laws increase the cost of private debt — lenders price the disclosure obligation as a systematic risk signal.

**Synthesis:** These three JBFA (N83, N84) and BAR (N88) papers collectively show that mandatory breach disclosure laws have unintended multi-market consequences: while disclosure improves breach-specific information transparency, it simultaneously creates strategic information asymmetry exploitation (insider trading), information withholding dynamics (bad news hoarding), and risk repricing (debt costs). For H2, this means the FCC/SEC rules' market effects are not limited to the announcement-day stock return — they reshape the firm's entire information environment, cost of capital, and managerial disclosure strategy. The FCC's 7-day incident notification rule (faster than most state laws) should compress the insider trading window (N83) and reduce hoarding incentives (N84), potentially mitigating these unintended consequences relative to the state-level laws these papers study.
