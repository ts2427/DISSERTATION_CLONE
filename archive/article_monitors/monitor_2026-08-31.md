## REGULATORY CONTEXT UPDATE — AUGUST 31, 2026

### Sixth Circuit Upholds FCC 2024 Data Breach Notification Rules (August 13, 2025)

**Ohio Telecom Association v. FCC**, No. 24-3133 (6th Cir. Aug. 13, 2025): A divided 2-1 Sixth Circuit panel upheld the FCC's 2024 data breach notification rules (the "Data Breach Order"), rejecting consolidated challenges from the Ohio Telecom Association, Texas Association of Business, and USTelecom. The court found that while 47 U.S.C. § 222(a) did not independently authorize the FCC to regulate personally identifiable information (PII), 47 U.S.C. § 201(b) — which prohibits unjust or unreasonable practices in the provision of communication services — independently supported the rule. The court also rejected the Congressional Review Act (CRA) challenge, holding the 2024 rule was not "substantially the same" as the CRA-disapproved 2016 rule.

**Status:** As of August 2025, petitioners filed for en banc rehearing (September 29, 2025), with amicus support from 24 members of Congress and 21 State Attorneys General. No en banc ruling has been reported.

**Dissertation relevance:** The Sixth Circuit ruling is the single most important legal development for H2. The court's affirmation means the FCC's 7-business-day notification requirement and expanded PII coverage are legally binding on telecom carriers — the regulatory regime H2 tests is now confirmed as durable. The dissent and en banc petition represent residual legal uncertainty that dissertation H2 findings should acknowledge.

**⚠️ Note:** This ruling is from August 2025 and does not appear to have been explicitly documented in prior monitor cycles. Monitor for en banc outcome (if any) and any law review analyses of the decision.

### Article N73 — NEW ⭐⭐ [2026 PUBLICATION]
**Bajpai, S., Pathak, J. & Yadav, K. (2026).** Cyber risk, 10-K report and audit fees. *Finance Research Letters*, 91, 109496.
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2026.109496
**ScienceDirect URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612326000279
**Published:** January 10, 2026
**⚠️ Note: Author first names not confirmed via publisher page — verify Shweta Bajpai, Jalaj Pathak, Kartik Yadav before dissertation citation.**

**Summary:** Bajpai, Pathak, and Yadav examine whether cybersecurity risk signals embedded in firms' 10-K narrative disclosures convey forward-looking information about future cyber incidents — and whether auditors incorporate these signals into audit pricing. Using a textual-similarity-based cyber risk index, they find that a one-standard-deviation rise in disclosure similarity raises the probability of a future cyber incident by 25% and increases audit fees by approximately 4.7% (approximately USD 130,000 per standard deviation change). Cyber-exposed firms are also significantly more likely to engage Big Four auditors. The core contribution is that latent cyber-risk signals in mandatory 10-K narratives are priced by auditors, demonstrating that the disclosure regime generates forward-looking risk assessments by third-party assurance providers.

**Relevance Analysis:**
- **H2 (FCC/SEC regulation → stock returns):** ⭐⭐ The audit fee finding provides indirect evidence that the SEC's mandatory 10-K cybersecurity disclosure framework produces economically meaningful third-party risk assessments — auditors, not just investors, price the cyber risk information in mandated disclosures. If mandatory disclosure (FCC 7-business-day, SEC Item 1.05) improves the signal quality of cyber risk information, auditors' pricing of that information can serve as a cross-validation mechanism for H2's market-reaction findings.
- **H5 (Volatility / information asymmetry):** ⭐⭐ Auditor risk pricing is a manifestation of information asymmetry costs borne by non-investor stakeholders — the 4.7% audit fee premium represents a real economic cost of cyber risk uncertainty. The finding that 10-K textual similarity predicts future incidents implies that the information asymmetry cost extends beyond equity markets to the audit market, broadening the scope of H5.
- **H1 (Firm characteristics):** The Big Four auditor engagement finding suggests that cyber-exposed firms seek higher-quality assurance as a governance signal — a firm-characteristics moderator for H1's disclosure-quality heterogeneity predictions. Larger firms with greater cyber exposure self-select into higher-quality audit relationships.
- **H2 / Haapamäki & Sihvonen (N48) pairing:** N48 found that prior breach history does not drive 10-K disclosure content, while N73 finds that the content that is disclosed is priced by auditors — together these papers show that 10-K narratives convey latent risk information auditors can extract even when the disclosure is not breach-triggered.
- **FLAG:** ⭐⭐ *Finance Research Letters (Elsevier) — peer-reviewed finance journal. 2026 publication. Author verification required. This is the first article in the monitor explicitly examining audit pricing of mandatory cybersecurity disclosures — a novel stakeholder beyond equity investors, providing a cross-market validation of the information content of mandatory disclosure. The 25% breach probability prediction from textual similarity is a methodological contribution that could be adapted for the dissertation's own breach risk measurement.*

### Article N74 — NEW ⭐⭐ [2025 PUBLICATION]
**Klumperbeek, S., Gadyatskaya, O. & Zohrehvand, A. (2025).** Strategic impression management: Pre-announcement social media discourse and market reactions to data breach announcements. In *Data Science and Artificial Intelligence. DSAI 2024. Communications in Computer and Information Science*, Vol. 2318, pp. 195–207. Springer, Singapore.
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-97-9793-6_15
**Springer URL:** https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-97-9793-6_15

**Summary:** Klumperbeek, Gadyatskaya, and Zohrehvand investigate whether firms engage in strategic social media impression management in the days before data breach announcements — and whether such pre-announcement discourse attenuates the negative market reaction. Using daily tweet data, they find a statistically significant surge in social media activity about firms in the 11-to-2-day window before breach announcements, consistent with strategic impression management. Firms with higher pre-announcement social media engagement experience smaller negative market reactions at announcement, suggesting that proactive disclosure management — even via informal social media channels — reduces the information asymmetry shock at formal announcement. The study directly operationalizes the pre-announcement information environment that N60 (Viancourt & Walkup) and N71 (Bangalore) document through financial data.

**Relevance Analysis:**
- **H1 (Disclosure timing and firm characteristics):** ⭐⭐ The pre-announcement social media surge is a firm-driven timing choice — a strategic behavior that varies across firms based on IR capacity, social media presence, and PR strategy. This cross-sectional variation in pre-announcement communication is a novel H1 moderator: firms that manage pre-announcement discourse more effectively receive smaller negative market reactions at the formal disclosure date. H1's timing dimension should account for both mandatory disclosure timing (FCC/SEC windows) and informal pre-announcement communication channels.
- **H5 (Information asymmetry):** ⭐⭐ The attenuation of negative market reactions by pre-announcement social media is direct evidence that pre-announcement information asymmetry is not only resolved by financial insiders (N60's institutional trading channel) but also by firm-initiated social media communications. This extends H5's information asymmetry framework to include the firm's own pre-disclosure communication as a moderator of the adverse-selection cost at announcement.
- **H2 (Regulation):** Mandatory disclosure rules (FCC 7-business-day, SEC 4-business-day) interact with firms' informal social media communication channels. If mandatory windows compress the pre-announcement period, the strategic impression management window also compresses — potentially reducing the attenuation effect N74 documents and increasing day-of-disclosure market impact. This is a novel H2/H5 interaction implication.
- **H4 (Breach type):** The pre-announcement social media strategy may differ by breach type — healthcare and financial data breaches (with higher reputational stakes) may attract more proactive impression management, moderating the H4 severity ordering in market reactions.
- **Pairing with N60 (Viancourt & Walkup, 2026, pre-announcement leakage):** N60 documents institutional trading before announcement; N74 documents firm-initiated social media before announcement. Together they establish a comprehensive pre-announcement information environment: both insider-driven (N60) and firm-driven (N74) channels resolve information asymmetry before the mandatory disclosure date. This joint finding reinforces the H2/H5 redesign noted in August 24 — mandatory disclosure's primary value is in compressing the pre-announcement period, not in the day-of-disclosure price impact.
- **FLAG:** ⭐⭐ *Springer CCIS volume from DSAI 2024 conference — peer-reviewed conference proceedings, not a journal article. The chapter is from a data science conference, not a finance or IS journal. Treat as corroborating evidence for H1/H5 pre-announcement findings rather than primary empirical anchor. However, the social media impression management channel is novel and not previously documented in the monitor — it represents a distinct information transmission mechanism worth including in the literature review.*

### Article N75 — NEW ⭐⭐ [2026 PUBLICATION]
**Agarwal, A., Lee, B. & Misra, S. (2026).** CMOs' influence on data breach prevention. *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science*.
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-026-01162-4
**Springer URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-026-01162-4

**Summary:** Agarwal, Lee, and Misra examine the role of Chief Marketing Officers (CMOs) in reducing the probability of data breaches, using both primary and secondary data across a large sample of public firms. They find that CMOs reduce breach probability partially through customer orientation, and quantify the effect as a 33-percentage-point reduction in breach probability associated with CMO presence. Board learning from breach prevalence at interlocked firms and the firm's marketing intensity enhance CMO effectiveness, while the presence of a technology oversight committee and CEO overconfidence diminish it. The paper establishes CMO presence as a novel C-suite governance variable in data breach prevention, distinct from CISO-focused governance studies.

**Relevance Analysis:**
- **H6 (Executive turnover response):** ⭐⭐ The CMO finding extends the H6 executive-turnover literature in two directions: (1) CMO absence or departure increases breach probability by 33 percentage points — suggesting that CMO turnover is itself a data breach risk factor; and (2) the CEO overconfidence moderator (which diminishes CMO effectiveness) implies that CEO characteristics interact with C-suite governance configurations to determine breach outcomes, consistent with H6's prediction that executive team composition shapes governance response to cyber risk. H6 should consider whether CMO departure rates change following major breaches alongside CEO, CIO, and CISO turnover.
- **H1 (Firm characteristics):** ⭐⭐ Board learning from interlocked firms and marketing intensity as CMO effectiveness moderators are firm-level characteristics directly relevant to H1. Firms with high marketing intensity (consumer-facing businesses) and boards with cybersecurity experience from interlocked firms have significantly different breach exposures — a novel H1 heterogeneity dimension beyond the standard size/leverage/sector moderators.
- **H4 (Breach type):** The customer orientation mechanism suggests CMOs are most effective at preventing consumer-data breaches (where customer relationship and trust management is central). This implies breach type interacts with CMO presence — customer data breaches in high-marketing-intensity firms may have different H4 severity profiles than technical/financial data breaches in non-consumer-facing firms.
- **H2 (Regulation):** The technology oversight committee moderator (which *diminishes* CMO effectiveness) suggests that governance structures mandated or encouraged by SEC disclosure rules (board cybersecurity oversight committees, required under Item 1C) may have unintended consequences on C-suite coordination around breach prevention. This is a novel H2 mechanism: mandatory governance disclosure requirements may inadvertently reduce CMO influence by formalizing cybersecurity as a CISO/technology-committee domain.
- **Pairing with N72 (CISO literature review, August 24):** N72 established CISO turnover and accountability as a distinct H6 dimension; N75 adds CMO presence as a breach-prevention variable with a customer orientation mechanism. Together they establish a multi-C-suite governance framework for H6: CISO (accountability/turnover), CMO (breach prevention/customer orientation), CEO (overconfidence/risk-taking incentives), and CIO (technical oversight).
- **FLAG:** ⭐⭐ *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science (JAMS, Springer) — top-tier marketing journal, 2026. First CMO-focused data breach paper in the monitor. The 33-percentage-point breach probability reduction is a striking effect size. The customer orientation mechanism provides a novel non-technical governance channel complementary to the CISO literature. Recommended for inclusion in the H6 governance section as the CMO-specific anchor.*

## KEY NEW INSIGHTS THIS CYCLE

### Multi-Stakeholder Information Asymmetry Framework — H5 Extended to Audit Market
- **N73 (Bajpai et al., 2026, FRL):** Auditors price cybersecurity risk in 10-K narratives — 4.7% fee premium, 25% breach probability prediction
- **N63 (Kelton & Yang, 2025, prior):** Investor-side information asymmetry reduced by strong internal controls
- **N60 (Viancourt & Walkup, 2026, prior):** Institutional trading before announcement resolves investor-side asymmetry

**New synthesis (August 31):** H5's information asymmetry costs now span three distinct stakeholder groups — equity investors (N60), institutional traders (N60), and auditors (N73). The audit market finding is particularly valuable because audit fees are contractually set and represent a binding economic cost of cyber risk uncertainty (unlike bid-ask spreads, which are transient). The 4.7% fee premium converts to approximately USD 130,000 per standard deviation of cyber risk — a concrete dollar figure for H5's information asymmetry cost that complements the market-capitalization loss estimates (N69: USD 309.33M average market value loss) in the dissertation's economic magnitude discussion.

### Pre-Announcement Information Environment — Now 3 Channels:
- **N60 (Viancourt & Walkup, 2026):** Institutional trading before announcement (market-side, insider-driven)
- **N71 (Bangalore, 2026, pre-registered):** No tradeable abnormal return at mandatory 8-K filing date (information resolved before filing)
- **N74 (Klumperbeek et al., 2025):** Firm-initiated social media surge 11–2 days before announcement (firm-side, strategic)

**New synthesis (August 31):** The pre-announcement information environment is resolved through at least three mechanisms: firm-initiated social media (N74), institutional trading (N60), and informal information leakage (N71). Mandatory disclosure rules (FCC 7-day, SEC 4-day) primarily compress the window during which these mechanisms operate — their value is not in the day-of-disclosure price impact (N71 null) but in reducing the duration of adverse selection. H2's empirical design should measure the pre-announcement window length as the primary outcome variable of the FCC/SEC rule.

### C-Suite Governance Cluster for H6 — Now 4 Executive Roles:
- **CEO:** Risk-taking incentives, compensation changes post-breach (prior monitors)
- **CIO:** Technical oversight, turnover following major incidents (prior monitors)
- **CISO:** Accountability structure, 18-26 month average tenure, scapegoating following breaches (N72, August 24)
- **CMO:** Customer orientation mechanism, 33-percentage-point breach probability reduction (N75, this cycle)

**New synthesis (August 31):** H6 can now distinguish four executive roles with distinct mechanisms and turnover patterns following breaches. CEO turnover is driven by overall financial accountability (broad performance link); CIO turnover by technical failure (breach prevention failure); CISO turnover by accountability scapegoating; CMO turnover (or absence) as a *predictor* of breach probability rather than a *response* to it. The CEO overconfidence × CMO effectiveness interaction (N75) provides a causal link between CEO characteristics and breach prevention governance — a novel H6/H1 intersection.
